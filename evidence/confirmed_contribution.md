# Confirmed contribution

## Mother object

The paper studies **source-indexed answer control**: which described source
governs a model's answer when the latent world is fixed but linguistic access
and typed competitors change.

## Central tension

False-belief accuracy identifies the winning answer, not the controller that
made it win. Pooled prompt effects are therefore non-identifying and can reverse
across item pools.

## Contribution

1. A typed conflict intervention that makes source control behaviorally
   identifiable under fixed semantics.
2. A source-accessibility cost whose additive null predicts unseen compound
   constructions.
3. Independent confirmation of subadditive composition in
   Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
4. An affine-gain invariance proposition and frozen checkpoint comparisons that
   show the composition boundary is functional rather than scalar confidence
   compression.

## Evidence spine

Pooled effects fail -> typed topology resolves the instability -> a
reportative reversal localizes the explanation to source access -> primitive
access restrictions predict held-out compounds -> new worlds confirm
subadditivity -> new checkpoints delimit the law and reject affine scaling.

## Claim boundary

The evidence supports a checkpoint-sensitive behavioral theory of linguistic
source routing. It does not establish a unique neural operator, hidden-state
belief circuit, causal effect of instruction tuning, broad model-family law, or
human psycholinguistic universal.

## Why LP4FM should care

The work shows that linguistic representation is not merely content supplied to
a model. Discourse form, reference, voice, embedding locality, and source
attribution act as a control interface over which described state reaches the
answer. The compositional behavior of that interface is measurable,
falsifiable, and checkpoint-sensitive.
